Program · Crisis communication

Happy employees, happy customers

Media Communication and Public Relations · National University of Singapore · Aug 2024

Case
Amazon's employee burnout crisis
My role
Team leader; video concept, script and editing
Result
Winning team, Final Presentation; individual Distinction

The program at NUS covered social media, public relations and crisis communication, and ended with a team case. Ours was the backlash Amazon faced over employee burnout, which had sparked media debate and put the company's reputation at risk.

We looked at how Amazon was responding on Facebook, Twitter, YouTube and its own website, and defined a target audience using Numerator's 2023 Amazon Shoppers Report. The gaps were clear: benefits were explained in vague terms, the apology came late, comments were shut down, and negative posts went unanswered. We recommended YouTube as the main channel, with a video in which Amazon shows it has heard its employees, explains the benefits it offers, and commits to making them satisfied, healthy and happy. We saw the video as one part of the PR response, not a solution to the burnout itself. Real change had to come from Amazon's policies and workplace, and the video's job was to communicate that change clearly.

I made that video. The idea came from watching the documentary Amazon Empire. I noticed that in Amazon's ads and social media, the camera almost always rests on the box itself. So the video starts with the box, then turns to the two people on either side of it: the employee who sends it and the customer who receives it. From there, three lines follow the same pattern, changing only one word each time.

Satisfied employees, satisfied customers.

Healthy employees, healthy customers.

Happy employees, happy customers.

I wrote the script and edited the one-to-two-minute video myself, using footage from Amazon's official YouTube channel. Our team won the program's Final Presentation competition, and I received a Distinction for the program.
