Reflections from making these videos

A few questions I keep asking myself.

Note 01

Does AI make my videos better, or just faster?

AI saves me hours. It organizes my messy notes, fills in what I'm curious about and helps me cut faster. But when I watch some of my videos back, they feel a little plastic. Everything is in the right place, yet the thing that makes people stay is missing. I think it lives in the small, specific moments that only come from my own life. I haven't found a way to keep them while still working fast, and it's the question I keep coming back to.

Note 02

Why does the same video travel so differently?

I post the same video on several platforms, and each one treats it differently. WeChat Channels is the steadiest: its recommendations are reliable, so every video gets a similar baseline of views. Xiaohongshu responds much more to how viewers react, so one video can take off while the next quietly stops. Bilibili has been the hardest. As a new creator, starting from zero there has felt almost impossible.

Note 03

More people saw it. Why didn't more people watch?

I once spent about ¥30 on a paid boost, and the distribution clearly worked: the video was shown to many more people. That's when I realized I had been mixing up two different things. Being recommended only puts my video in front of someone. Whether they tap on it is decided in a split second, mostly by the cover. Click-through rate was the gap I hadn't been paying attention to.

Note 04

Polished or real?

So I started paying attention to covers. At first I used AI to design polished covers that matched each video. Then some viewers told me they looked too AI-made, which made the video feel less genuine. I switched to simple covers with just a line of text. They felt more honest, but clicks seemed to drop a little. I don't have the answer yet.
