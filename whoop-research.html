What makes people renew WHOOP?

A mixed-methods study of membership value, AI coaching and everyday wear for a subscription wearable. I led it from research design to final recommendations.

The business problem

WHOOP sells a membership, not a one-time device, so it lives or dies on retention: members need a reason to renew after the novelty of tracking fades. We set out to learn what keeps them paying, across three questions: membership value, AI coaching and everyday wear.

How I led the research

Four methods in sequence, each one built on what the last uncovered. I led every stage and every key decision.

The core insight

People don't keep paying to be measured.
They keep paying when the data answers a question they already have and points to one clear next step.

What we tested, and what we found

Survey results on 1–5 scales. With this sample size, they are directional rather than statistically significant.

RQ1 · Membership value

Which membership would people keep paying for?

What we tested: respondents rated three membership concepts, each built around a different promise.

Performance · training and recovery decisions
Everyday Wellness · simple sleep, stress and habit guidance, fewer notifications
Long-Term Health · healthspan and change over months and years

Likely to keep using it long term (n = 19)

Everyday Wellness4.26
Long-Term Health4.05
Performance3.89

Everyday Wellness led overall, but people who train three or more times a week preferred the other two.
Value follows each person's goal.

RQ2 · AI coaching and guidance

How should the data talk to people?

What we tested: three ways of showing the same daily result, and when people want guidance at all.

A · numbers only
B · numbers plus an explanation
C · numbers plus one next step

"Helps me take a next step" (n = 17)

C · plus next step3.94
B · plus explanation3.65
A · numbers only3.29
71% want guidance only when something important changes. No one chose "no guidance."

Numbers alone were easiest to read; one action made them useful. The data is trusted, but often not useful.

RQ3 · Everyday wear

What keeps it on the wrist, 24/7?

What we tested: how much six factors matter when wearing a device day and night, from comfort and battery to style, materials and personalization.

4.18
battery, ranked first
4.00
comfort, ranked second

Interviewees praised the light device and long battery, but found the default band slow to dry. The device fits 24/7 wear; the band is the weak point.

What I recommended

  1. i.Frame the membership around each person's goal and life stage, whether training, rest or long-term health.
  2. ii.Keep the number, add one action, and stay quiet unless something unexpected happens.
  3. iii.Make a quick-drying band the default for true 24/7 wear.

What I took from leading it

Leading the project meant making calls, not just dividing tasks: which questions mattered, which groups to talk to, and when our own sample wasn't enough.

The biggest lesson was to be honest about the limits of the data. Noticing that our sample skewed young, and adding public user voices to cover the gap, made the recommendations stronger than any single method could.
